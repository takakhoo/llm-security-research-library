Title: Dual Use Concerns of Generative AI and Large Language Models
Authors: Alexei Grinbaum, Laurynas Adomaitis (CEA-Saclay/Larsim)
Venue: Journal of Responsible Innovation, 2024 (arXiv:2305.07882, 2023)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07882
GitHub: (none)

Summary:
The paper proposes adapting the Dual Use Research of Concern (DURC) framework, developed by life-sciences governance to manage research that could be repurposed for harm (e.g., gain-of-function virology), to the governance of generative AI and LLMs. The authors trace DURC's history in biosecurity, identify which structural features transfer to AI (capability uplift, asymmetric attacker/defender cost, ambiguity of intent) and which do not (replication economics, tacit-knowledge bottlenecks). They argue that an AI-DURC regime should classify dual-use risks by capability tier rather than by domain, distinguishing model-development decisions, deployment decisions, and downstream user actions, and they sketch institutional mechanisms (review boards, structured access, conditional release). The paper is policy-flavored rather than technical and explicitly notes the trade-offs DURC has accumulated in the bio context (overreach, chilling effects, leak failure modes). Defenders and policy teams use it as a conceptual scaffold when arguing for tiered model release, third-party safety evals, and pre-deployment review processes that are grounded in an existing, legally familiar dual-use lineage rather than invented from scratch.
