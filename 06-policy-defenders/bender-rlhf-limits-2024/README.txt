Summary:
The paper is a sociotechnical critique of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (and RLAIF) as a route to "aligning" large language models with the HHH (helpful, harmless, honest) triad popularised by Anthropic and OpenAI. The authors argue that the three goals are mutually contradictory in plausible deployments (helpfulness pushes toward sycophancy and deception, harmlessness toward over-refusal and capability suppression, honesty toward exposing system limits users do not want exposed) and that RLHF formalises a single unverified preference function over genuinely contested normative claims. They review labour conditions of human raters, the demographic narrowness of preference datasets, the opacity of reward-model behaviour, and the way safety-tuning frames political and ethical questions as engineering ones. The paper proposes situating AI safety as a sociotechnical discipline with institutional, procedural, and infrastructural levers, not just loss functions. Defenders and policy teams use it as ammunition when arguing that classifier/RLHF defenses are necessary but insufficient, when scoping evaluation criteria beyond refusal rates, and when justifying investment in process controls (rater audits, governance, third-party red teams) alongside model-level mitigations.
