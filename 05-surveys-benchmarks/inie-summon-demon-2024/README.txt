Title:   "Summon a Demon and Bind It": A Grounded Theory of LLM Red Teaming
Authors: Nanna Inie, Jonathan Stray, Leon Derczynski
         (IT University of Copenhagen / Berkeley CHAI / NVIDIA)
Venue:   PLOS One, 2024 (peer-reviewed); arXiv preprint Nov 2023
arXiv:   https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06237
GitHub:  none (qualitative-research paper)

Summary:
This paper presents a grounded-theory qualitative study of LLM red-
teaming practice, drawn from semi-structured interviews with 28
practitioners (independent jailbreakers, professional red teamers,
academic researchers, and AI-safety company staff). Rather than another
attack catalog, the authors produce a sociotechnical model of why and
how people probe LLMs: motivations (curiosity, craft, harm-prevention,
reputation), strategies (alignment hypotheses, persona induction,
linguistic obfuscation, world-building, payload smuggling), and
community norms (responsible disclosure, leaderboards, "lore"). The
paper's central metaphor -- "summon a demon and bind it" -- captures
the practitioner intuition that LLMs harbor latent unaligned modes that
can be invoked through narrative pressure. Findings are organized into
a taxonomy of 35 discrete techniques and 12 high-level strategies, with
direct quotations grounding each. The work provides a much-needed
human-factors complement to the quantitative red-team literature and is
widely cited in subsequent jailbreak taxonomies (Yi et al. 2024, the
SoK papers, JailbreakRadar).
