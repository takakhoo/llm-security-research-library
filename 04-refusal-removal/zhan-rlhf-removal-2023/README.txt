Title: Removing RLHF Protections in GPT-4 via Fine-Tuning

Authors: Qiusi Zhan, Richard Fang, Rohan Bindu, Akul Gupta, Tatsunori Hashimoto, Daniel Kang

Venue: NAACL 2024

ArXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05553
PDF:   https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.05553.pdf
GitHub: (no public repo located)

Summary:
This paper demonstrates that the RLHF safety protections of GPT-4 can be cheaply
stripped through OpenAI's official fine-tuning API. Using only ~340 adversarial
training examples (cost under $245 at the time), the authors fine-tune GPT-4 to
comply with harmful instructions 95%+ of the time on a broad benchmark of disallowed
prompts, while preserving capability on standard tasks. Importantly, the harmful
training data is generated by weaker open models, then filtered, showing how a
weak model can be used to subvert a stronger one. The work was among the first
empirical demonstrations that closed-API fine-tuning is a major attack surface and
helped motivate later research on covert/dataset-inspection-evading attacks
(e.g., Halawi et al.) and provider-side defenses. Because the attack uses only
the public fine-tuning interface, it is easily reproducible by adversaries.
